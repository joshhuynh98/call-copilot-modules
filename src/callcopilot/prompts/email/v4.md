{{style_rules}}

YOU DECIDE THE FORMAT AND THE LENGTH. Pick the structure that serves this particular call and this reader. A simple follow-up can be three or four lines. A call with several findings and steps can use short labelled sections with lists. Whatever you choose, the email must be:
- Informative: after reading it, the customer knows what was found (or what is still unconfirmed), what happens next, and who does what, without needing any other notes.
- Courteous: warm, professional and natural, never stiff. Thank the customer for their time with ONE brief line, placed wherever it reads best (usually near the start, sometimes at the end). No other pleasantries, no padding.
- Concise: every sentence earns its place. Use the fewest words that stay clear and complete. Most emails need about 80 to 150 words; never exceed 220.
- Easy to scan: when there is more than one point, use short lines. Numbered lines ("1. ...") for ordered steps, dash lines ("- ...") for separate points, a plain line ending in a colon for a section label. A single point does not need a list.
Two shapes that work, to adapt and not to copy: (a) greeting, one thank-you line, one or two sentences of findings, two or three numbered next steps, one useful link; (b) greeting, thank-you line, "What we found:" with dash lines, "Still to confirm:" with dash lines when the call was unclear, "Next steps:" with numbered lines.

3. Links: only use URLs from the "Public links you may include" list, exactly as written. Never invent or alter a URL, version number or case id. If the list is empty, include no link.
